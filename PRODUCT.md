# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Crypto-native savers: people who already hold a wallet, some $WIF and USDG on Robinhood Chain, and who know (or are) people who save in an ajo / esusu / susu / chama. Many arrive on a phone through an invite link a friend sent. A second, short-lived audience: the World is Flat hackathon judges, who will try the live app between 12 and 16 Nov 2026.

The job: save together with people you know, on a schedule, without trusting one organiser to hold the money. On any screen the user wants to know: what do I owe, by when, who has paid, whose turn is it, and what do I do next.

## Product Purpose

Ajo runs a rotating savings circle with a contract. Members pay the same USDG amount every round; each round one member takes the whole pot, in joining order. Every member locks a $WIF bond; miss a round and the bond goes to the members who kept paying. Success: circles that finish with everyone paid out, and members who come back to start the next one.

## Brand Personality

Futuristic, crypto-native, trustworthy. Retro-pixel and arcade-adjacent in look (see the reference: dark grid, acid lime, purple, blocky pixel type), but calm in behaviour: it holds people's savings. Plain words for money ("pay", "pot", "bond", "withdraw"); crypto words like wallet and onchain are fine without explanation.

## Anti-references

- A casino or degen token site: no hype copy, no flashing, no "ape in", no moon countdowns.
- A bank app: no stiff navy-and-white corporate look.
- A copy of CyberKongz (the visual reference): same style family, but our own name, art and layout. No apes.
- Anything hard to read on a phone.

## Design Principles

1. Money first, style second: amounts, deadlines and the next action must be unmistakable on every screen, whatever the aesthetic does.
2. Say what is true right now: show live chain state; never imply a payout or refund that has not happened.
3. One obvious next step: each screen leads with the single action that matters for this user at this moment.
4. Playful look, serious behaviour: pixels and colour carry personality; motion and copy stay calm.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 contrast for text, visible focus on every control, 44px touch targets.
- Phone-first readability: sentences and amounts at 16px or larger; small uppercase labels never carry information that is not also said elsewhere.
- Status is never colour alone: every state badge has words.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
